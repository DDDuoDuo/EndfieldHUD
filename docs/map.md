# Map

Map shares the retained circular HUD, perspective, theme and section transitions. It combines offline NOAA ETOPO 2022 relief with raised Natural Earth country plates, shaded sides, bright outlines and diagonal hatching. Only the country beneath the map center uses the HUD theme color; moving the pointer does not change country highlighting. Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macao are grouped into the China plate for this application's requested display. See [terrain provenance and preparation](map-data.md) for sources, presentation grouping, licenses and resolution.

- Drag with the left mouse button to pan. Longitude wraps across the date line; latitude stops at the poles.
- Click empty terrain to bring that position to the center without changing zoom, subject to the same polar bounds. Small pointer jitter within three design points remains a click. Leaving the section cancels a pending click.
- Scroll or pinch to zoom around the pointer. The `+` and `−` controls zoom around the center, from 2.1× to 128×. The minimum magnification and latitude bounds keep rectangular map edges outside the circular viewport, even near the poles.
- The initial view centers on Shenzhen at 3×. The crosshair resets zoom to 3× while retaining the current geographic center, subject to the polar bounds. Schema 4 upgrades the original version-1 overview and unchanged version-2/3 default cameras once; it preserves custom version-2/3 views and every pin. Subsequent launches retain the user's saved view. Visible place labels and gesture instructions are omitted; accessibility help retains the gesture instructions.
- Right-click terrain to place a pin. The ring control on the right also places one at the map center.
- Click a pin to cycle its appearance: yellow, green, then the player arrow and beam. Its position, identity and creation time stay unchanged. The small `×` or Delete removes the selected pin. Right-clicking an existing pin removes it instead of stacking another at the same point. Escape clears selection before closing the HUD. Accessibility activation also cycles a pin and exposes its coordinates.
- Arrow keys move the map. Plus/minus keys change zoom. Controls and visible pins expose native accessibility actions.

Yellow and warm yellow-green pins have a solid center and steady ring while a thin ring expands and fades. The player marker uses the original Watch arrow, halo and beam texture. Up to 12 visible markers pulse at once; the selected marker gets priority. Other markers stay static. Reduce Motion, disabled ambient animation and Low Power visual mode suppress these pulses. All map animations stop when its section is inactive or the HUD closes.

Up to 128 pins, their styles and creation times, and the viewport are stored atomically in `~/Library/Application Support/EndfieldCharge/WorldMap/map.json`. The optional `style` field extends schema 4; earlier pins default to yellow without rewriting an existing schema-4 archive on load. Coordinates stay tied to the terrain while panning or zooming. The viewport saves when a gesture ends, rather than on every input event. Stored pins are never silently evicted when the limit is reached. Diagnostic `--ui-test` launches use a separate temporary store. Malformed archives and external edits are preserved rather than overwritten. Successful explicit style changes and recentering produce application Event Log entries containing only the action and style, never pin IDs or coordinates.

The bundled terrain is about 602 KB. Its 142,392 vertices and the bounded country geometry are decoded once on a utility queue into shared vector paths. A serial background worker clips and simplifies visible geometry, then draws relief, outlines, contours and hatching into one padded viewport image, capped at 1536 pixels per edge. Pointer input immediately transforms this image and the independently projected pins. Repainting is coalesced and cost-throttled during gestures, with a full-detail image after movement settles. Three wrap layers share a small world backdrop for coverage during rapid movement. There is no idle render loop or unbounded image history. No map downloads, runtime contour generation, polling or display-rate sampling run in the app. Pin pulses use Core Animation. The map does not access location services or track the user's location. This is generalized global terrain: zooming does not load street-level detail.

Only the country beneath the camera center receives the theme-colored face. This restores the renderer from release commit `747ca57`: the highlight, relief and hatching are painted together in the existing bounded terrain image. Pointer movement does not query countries, create country layers or schedule a second bitmap renderer. Center changes become part of the next coalesced terrain frame, with exact detail when a camera gesture settles.

The experimental pointer-highlight plates, candidate grid and separate country bitmap cache have been removed. The map retains the original four raster layers (three shared world-backdrop copies and one detail image), plus existing chrome and pin layers. The updated yellow, green and player pin artwork, selection behavior, click-to-recenter and stored style fields remain intact. Marker layers use three shared decoded source images; the beam has no separate timer or render loop.

## Isolated renderer check

On 2026-10-04, the restored center-only renderer completed seven optimized offscreen scenarios on Apple M2 / macOS 15.7.4: 120 camera updates and 12 exact-image draws per scenario. Camera-update/enqueue p95 was 0.021–0.033 ms; this is not total frame latency. Mean forced Core Graphics draw time was 30.0–51.5 ms and exact-image waiting was 104.9–218.2 ms, including gesture-end settling. All settled scenarios retained five raster layers including their root, sharing two images. Process CPU across measured intervals was 9.243 seconds over 15.634 elapsed seconds.

Reproduce with `MAP_PERFORMANCE_DIR="$PWD/build/map-center-only-benchmark" scripts/benchmark-map.sh --build-only`, then, outside other builds/tests, `build/map-center-only-benchmark/WorldMapPerformance --label center-only-final --frames 120 --render-frames 12 --output "$PWD/build/map-center-only-benchmark/report.json"`. Local output includes `report.json`, `run.log` and a source/binary-hash `manifest.json`. This was one unpaced, no-GUI run using temporary map data. It measures neither live HUD CPU/FPS, compositor/GPU work, idle behavior nor painter working-memory bounds; there was no baseline comparison and no speedup claim. [Measurement details](map-performance.md#center-only-rollback-check--2026-10-04) distinguish image waiting from synchronous drawing.

The original `RegionMapPlayer/PlayerArrow` supplies the glyph. `deco_readio_mask` supplies the halo at its authored 0.149 opacity. The source `RegionMapPlayer/Equipring` uses the grayscale ramp in the `M_fx_regionmap_player` additive material. The native map applies that material's yellow RGB `(1, 0.9898965359, 0.3056603670)` once to the decoded beam image in memory. Each pixel retains grayscale-times-yellow premultiplied RGB, with grayscale alpha so the black end is transparent. A narrow retained layer projects the beam above the arrow and uses screen blending for its additive footprint. This adapts the original mesh/material to the map's flat Core Animation surface; it does not run the source 3D scene, add a live image filter or write generated artwork. All three cached images are bounded to 256 pixels per edge and prepared once, including missing-resource results.
